Estimating Net Migration in Italy, 1981–2021

An undergraduate demography thesis using census data to estimate migration balances among Italy's foreign-born residents.

Problem / Objective

Estimate how net migration among foreign-born residents in Italy changed between 1981 and 2021, and examine differences by age, sex and region of birth. The research separates changes in the resident population from migration balances estimated through demographic accounting.

Data

Census population counts for 1981, 1991, 2001, 2011 and 2021, with elaborations based on Istat and Eurostat data. The population of interest is foreign-born residents, which is not equivalent to foreign citizens. The analysis covers age and sex distributions and seven regions of birth. Table 1 reports an increase from 1.0647 million foreign-born residents in 1981 to 6.1610 million in 2021.

Methods

The thesis applies a modified intercensal projection method. Observed population changes are reconciled with estimated deaths and movement across age boundaries to infer net migration indirectly. Person-years and age-boundary quantities are approximated using geometric means. Descriptive age profiles, demographic indices and comparisons across birth regions complement the migration estimates. The methodological discussion draws on Hill (1987) and Strozza (2015).

Results

Table 7 reports estimated net migration of 472,600 in 1981–1991, 894,600 in 1991–2001, 2,711,800 in 2001–2011 and 1,485,100 in 2011–2021. The largest estimated balance occurs in 2001–2011, followed by a marked reduction in the final decade. The total reported estimate is approximately 5.56 million over the forty-year period. Separately, Table 3 reports an increase in the foreign-born population's mean age from 32.8 to 42.6 years. These are demographic estimates and descriptive changes, not counts of all individual arrivals.

Limitations

Residual estimates depend on census comparability, mortality assumptions and approximations to movement across age groups. They cannot separately identify gross immigration and emigration. Birthplace and citizenship must remain distinct. Several regional and sex-disaggregated totals elsewhere in the thesis do not reconcile with the aggregate table; the chart uses Table 7 consistently and does not combine conflicting breakdowns. Estimates are reproduced from the thesis rather than independently recalculated.

My contribution

Sole-author undergraduate thesis by Angelo Esposito, supervised by Professor Salvatore Strozza at the University of Naples Federico II, Political Science degree programme, academic year 2024/2025. My work is the demographic analysis and written interpretation presented in the thesis.

Tools

Demographic accounting · census-data analysis · indirect estimation · age and sex profiles. The thesis does not identify the analysis software.
