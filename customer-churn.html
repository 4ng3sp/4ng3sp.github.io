Customer Churn & Retention Strategy

Comparing classifiers and translating churn probabilities into a cost-sensitive retention policy in KNIME.

Problem / Objective

Which customers are most likely to leave, and when is a retention offer economically justified? This project combines customer-level analysis, classification and a simple intervention-cost model to move from risk scores to a contact policy.

Data

Two input tables containing customer demographics and service, contract and payment information were joined on customerID. The merged table contains 7,043 customers and 22 columns. The binary target is Churn (Yes/No). Derived tenure and monthly-charge groups support exploratory analysis. The saved random 70/30 split contains 4,930 training and 2,113 test observations (seed 123).

Methods

The workflow checks duplicates and missing values, creates descriptive segments, encodes categorical variables and normalises numerical inputs. It compares Gaussian-regularised logistic regression, a decision tree and a random forest using the same split. The forest uses 100 trees, maximum depth 10 and minimum child-node size 20. ROC/AUC guides discrimination assessment. The operational rule contacts customers with predicted churn probability at least 0.20. The economic scenario assumes €1.50 per contact, €15 loss per churned customer and 50% intervention effectiveness.

Results

The workflow annotations report AUCs of 0.844 for logistic regression, 0.823 for random forest and 0.818 for the decision tree. They report approximately 86.5% churn recall and a 34.2% false-positive rate around the 0.20 operating threshold. The retention calculation uses 489 identified churners: at assumed 50% effectiveness, 244.5 expected retained customers correspond to €3,667.50 in avoided loss before campaign costs. This is a scenario estimate, not realised revenue or a measured causal effect. The model outputs have been read from the saved workflow, not independently rerun.

Limitations

Normalisation is saved before the train/test partition, so preprocessing must be refitted on training data alone before claiming a clean holdout estimate. Model and threshold selection on the same evaluation split can introduce optimism. One annotation mentions 0.201, while the executable rule uses >= 0.20; this page follows the rule. The 50% effectiveness assumption is untested, and the monetary amounts are simplified business-case inputs. No net ROI percentage is claimed without a verified campaign-cost total.
