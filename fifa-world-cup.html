Statistical Modelling of the 2022 FIFA World Cup

An interpretable model of goal difference, with diagnostics, robust estimation and a random-forest benchmark.

Problem / Objective

Explain goal difference across the 64 matches of the 2022 FIFA World Cup and assess whether a more flexible random forest materially improves prediction over a parsimonious linear model. The intended use is explanation and pre-match prediction, not a claim of reliable betting profitability.

Data

The match is the unit of analysis (n = 64). Goal difference is goals for the team labelled home minus goals for the team labelled away; these are dataset labels, not a conventional home-ground advantage. Sources combined in the report include match data and betting odds, the FIFA ranking dated 6 October 2022, previous World Cup results and player records from the worldcup package. Candidate predictors cover market-implied probabilities, prior performance, tournament stage, history and player age.

Methods

Multiple linear regression is developed through substantive variable selection, VIF checks, backward comparisons and nested-model assessment. The final specification includes the difference in Shin-adjusted implied win probabilities, a binary PPG-advantage indicator and knockout stage. Diagnostics examine residuals, leverage and Cook's distance; Huber and Tukey robust fits assess sensitivity. Prediction is assessed with leave-one-out and 10-fold cross-validation. A 500-tree random forest (mtry = 2) uses the same three final predictors.

Results

The final OLS model reports R² = 0.3178 and adjusted R² = 0.2837. The implied-probability coefficient is 2.0231 (95% interval 1.0764–2.9699), and the knockout coefficient is 1.2075 (0.2772–2.1377). The PPG indicator's interval crosses zero. Reported 10-fold RMSE is 1.7001 goals for OLS and 1.6967 for random forest: a negligible difference in these estimates. This supports choosing the simpler model for interpretation, without establishing that the underlying relationship is truly linear. Spain–Costa Rica is an influential match, with observed goal difference 7 and fitted value about 1.60.

Limitations

There are only 64 matches, and teams appear repeatedly. Random folds do not establish performance in a new tournament or account automatically for within-team dependence. The report does not establish that feature selection was repeated inside every validation fold. Some narrative descriptions conflict with displayed tables, so this summary prioritises numerical output. The candidate age variable uses players who actually appeared, which requires a timing audit before any blanket no-leakage claim; it is absent from the final model. The definition and availability of PPG also need checking. Results are associations, not causal effects.

My contribution
